## Derrida

In writing the history of madness, Foucault wanted — and herein lies the whole value, as well as the impossibility, of his book — to write the history of madness itself. Itself. Madness itself.

## Baudrillard

everything strives to be realized more and more fully. One day the social will be realized completely - there will be no social isolation left. One day everything will become culturalized, everything, every object, will be a so-called aesthetic object, and then nothing at all will be an aesthetic object anymore.  
  
nullity is a mysterious quality to which not just anyone or anything can lay claim. insignificance, genuine nonsense - this is a challenge, a victorious challenge to meaning, to the poverty of meaning. the art of the disappearance of meaning is an exceptional quality, inherent only in a few of the rarest works, which made no such claim at all  
  
contemporary art plays on this uncertainty and indeterminacy, on this impossibility of any evaluative judgment regarding its aesthetic foundations, and speculates on the guilt of those who understand nothing in it, or who fail to understand that there is nothing to understand here

## Fichte

the not-I object limits the I subject  
  
The "not-I" limits the "I", but in doing so it imparts an impulse (Anstoss), a check on the I's infinite striving for mastery.  
  
The "I" strives to overcome this check. Within it awaken drives toward reflection — but reflection necessarily presupposes the realization of some object of representation — and the "I" manifests this striving toward productivity. Yet the I's attempt to embody its practical strivings, to slake its insatiable thirst for activity, runs up against the limitation posed by the "not-I". Hence dissatisfaction, a feeling of constraint; it gives rise to a striving toward self-determination. Self-determination must consist in freedom, in harmony between drive and its realization. Such harmony is attainable only through unceasing activity for the sake of activity, in which the "absolute drive" of our "I" is expressed — moral duty.

## Merleau-Ponty

**The body schema** — the body is not one object among objects but the vehicle of being-in-the-world. I do not *have* a body and steer it; I *am* my body, and motor intentionality ("I can") runs ahead of any representation. The shift is from consciousness to the lived body — the antidote to reading [Mamardashvili's effort](/doc/mamardashvili) as a purely intellectual act.

**Primacy of perception** — the perceiving body is already sense-making before judgment gets there; perception is not a degraded form of thinking.

**The flesh (chair)** — late, unfinished: perceiver and perceived are of one fabric, shown by the hand that touches while being touched.

The perceived "something" is always located within some kind of medium.  
Pure perception is impossible not only to find, but even to perceive.  
  
So, we would have to perceive some region of the world, marked out by sharp boundaries, surrounded by a certain zone of blackness, filled without gaps by qualities, sustained by those same determinate ratios of magnitudes that exist on the retina.

## Whitehead

Events strive toward the complication and ordering of their own experience. Creativity propels events. Eros and risk - events take risks. Toward the unknown. God is understood as yet another event. God carries potentialities. God is the organ of novelty. Deep ecology. Influenced: Graham Harman, Deleuze

## Protagoras (sophist)

Protagoras wrote a work that became the cause of his death — the treatise "On the Gods." From it the following phrase has come down to us: "Concerning the gods, I cannot know either that they exist, or that they do not exist, or what they are like in form. For many things prevent us from knowing this: both the obscurity of the question and the brevity of human life."  
  
"On every matter there are two opinions opposed to each other."  
  
"Since man lives in a particular 'linguistic reality' that does not reflect actuality faithfully, truth is formed in the space of human communication."  
  
My thought latched onto this. Reflecting reality is, one way or another, a reflection of one's own reality. This lets you sense that automatic contraband that has been smuggled into us and set off at the necessary moment. Contraband about how to reflect and carry something across in such a way that others, too, can reflect it. This utilitarianism hums within us, feeding the skill of survival, and accordingly, once that task of survival is accomplished, it drifts on in the form of enslaving other movements by this movement — or of reflection... One reflection for the sake of another, one for the other, being for the other.

## Husserl

The source of the method the rest of this line bends to its own ends.

**Intentionality** — consciousness is always consciousness *of* something; there is no contentless awareness. Taken from Brentano and turned into a working procedure.

**Epoché / the reduction** — bracket the question of whether the world exists and describe what is given, and how it is given. Suspension, not doubt.

**Noesis / noema** — the act of meaning and the object exactly as meant in it.

**The life-world (Lebenswelt)** — the pre-reflective, always already presupposed world of ordinary experience, out of which science later abstracts its idealized objects. The charge of the *Crisis*: science forgot the soil it grew from and mistook its abstraction for the thing itself.

## Sartre

**Being-in-itself / being-for-itself** — the in-itself is full, massive, what it is. The for-itself is consciousness: a nothingness that "is what it is not and is not what it is".

**Existence precedes essence** — no given nature waiting to be realized; a person is nothing but what he makes of himself, and is therefore condemned to be free.

**Bad faith (mauvaise foi)** — self-deception with no second person to do the deceiving: fleeing freedom by playing a thing with a fixed essence. The waiter who *is* a waiter; the woman who leaves her hand in his and treats it as an object. Almost the exact antipode of [effort](/doc/mamardashvili) — the refusal of the labor of choosing oneself.

**The look (le regard)** — the Other turns me into an object, and shame is the proof that the Other exists. Hence "hell is other people": not company, but being fixed as a thing in someone else's world.

## Kierkegaard

The historical ancestor of the whole line, [Heidegger](/doc/heidegger) and Sartre included.

**Stages of existence** — aesthetic (immediacy, the interesting; ends in the despair of boredom), ethical (choice, commitment, the universal), religious (faith, where the single individual stands above the universal — Abraham).

**Despair** — the self is a relation that relates to itself; despair is the misrelation, and it is the ordinary condition, mostly unnoticed. Its two forms: not wanting to be oneself, and wanting to be a self one is not.

**The leap of faith** — no continuity of reason bridges the gap. Truth is subjectivity: the passionate appropriation of an objective uncertainty.

**Anxiety** — the dizziness of freedom.

## Levinas

Against the whole line above.

**Ethics as first philosophy** — not being but the relation to the Other is primary. Ontology already reduces the other to the same, and that reduction is where violence starts.

**il y a ("there is")** — being without beings: impersonal, anonymous, an insomniac humming of existence that nauseates. Not peace at the bottom of things, and there is no getting out of it alone.

**The face (visage)** — the face of the Other is not a phenomenon to be grasped or described; it commands, *thou shalt not kill*. Responsibility is infinite, asymmetrical, and precedes any choice to accept it — I am hostage before I am free.

> The sharpest counterpoint to [Mamardashvili](/doc/mamardashvili): there, effort is solitary and the courage of the impossible presupposes fundamental solitude. Here nothing is saved alone.

## Simone Weil

Closest in intonation to [Mamardashvili's effort](/doc/mamardashvili), in an entirely different vocabulary — and pointed the other way: at emptying rather than at holding.

**Attention** — the highest effort and yet a negative one: not muscular straining but suspending thought, holding it empty and available, so the object can enter. "Attention, taken to its highest degree, is the same thing as prayer." Compare the exercise-shaped version in [gurdjieff](/doc/gurdjieff).

**Decreation** — undo the "I" that says I; consent to not existing, so that something other than oneself has room.

**Affliction (malheur)** — not suffering as such but suffering plus social degradation plus the wrecking of the self. It makes attention nearly impossible, and is therefore the test of it.

**Gravity and grace** — gravity is the natural downward mechanics of the soul; grace the only exception, and it fills nothing except a void that has been left for it.

## Pyatigorsky

Nearest of the contemporaries to [Mamardashvili](/doc/mamardashvili) by type of thinking; together they wrote **"Symbol and Consciousness"** (1982).

**Thinking is a rare event, not a permanent function** — most of the time no one is thinking. Thinking happens, and can equally fail to happen; philosophy is the observation of thinking, not a doctrine about it.

**Consciousness is not mine** — not a property of an individual but a sphere one enters or fails to enter. Consciousness is where a thought is thought, and it is describable only from a position taken up inside it.

**Symbol** — not a sign standing in for a meaning, but the thing through which consciousness becomes observable at all. Buddhology as method: take the observer's position seriously and stop pretending to a view from nowhere.
